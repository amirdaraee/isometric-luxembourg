# 03 – Underground: casemates, galleries and tunnels

Reference material for a possible "underground" layer of Isometric Luxembourg: what lies under Luxembourg City (fortress casemates and mine galleries, the Aquatunnel, road and rail tunnels, lifts, the funicular and underground car parks), where it is, how deep it goes and how it can be visited, plus the maps and GIS data to draw it from.

How to use this folder:
- `images/`: **22 historical maps and plans** (`*__plan-*`, `*__lefort-*`, `*__overlay-*`, `*__ferraris-*`) and **20 photos** of underground spaces not covered by tasks 1–2 (Olizy, Lambert, Bourbon, Redoute Marie, Fort Wallis, Aquatunnel, lift tunnels). Maps are full-size originals from Wikimedia Commons (most are Gallica scans, 6000–13000 px) unless noted in the table; photos are 1280–1920 px thumbnails. Commons rate-limited full-size downloads during collection, so 12 maps are reduced copies (3840, 1920 or 1280 px wide, marked in the table); re-download the originals from the source pages if more detail is needed.
- `sources.json`: one record per file with `subject`, `shows`, `date`, `source_page`, `author`, `licence`, `licence_url`. All maps are public domain; photos are CC BY / CC BY-SA (attribution needed if published; BY-SA derivatives share alike). The two data sets have their own licences (below).
- `data/`: GIS data (see [GIS data](#gis-data)).
- Bock and Pétrusse interior photos are already in `../01-landmarks/images/` and `../02-buildings-interiors/images/` (`bock-casemates__*`, `petrusse-casemates__*`) and are not repeated here.

## Overview

Luxembourg sits on a sandstone plateau (Luxembourg Sandstone) cut by the gorges of the Alzette and Pétrusse. The rock is soft enough to dig but stands without lining, so every ruler of the fortress tunnelled into it:

- **1644 onwards (Spanish):** first casemates in the Bock rock and under the Beck bastion (Pétrusse), by the engineer Isaac van Treybach.
- **1684–1697 (French, Vauban):** the city taken by Louis XIV; Vauban rebuilt the Bock and the outer works and treated underground passages as part of the defence. Detached forts on the Grünewald front (Obergrünewald hornwork, Niedergrünewald crownwork, Olizy).
- **1715–1795 (Austrian):** the biggest underground expansion. Engineers such as Simon de Bauffe and the commander Neipperg added outer forts (Thüngen, Olizy, Rubamprez, Rumigny, Neipperg, Wallis, Rheinsheim, Charles), the Bock casemates proper (1737–1746) and the Pétrusse battery (1746–1751), and dug **countermine networks** (listening and mine galleries ending in powder chambers) under the glacis of every front. Miners were brought from Austria to blast the galleries.
- **1815–1867 (Prussian garrison of the German Confederation):** modernisation (Fort Thüngen towers 1836–37, Louvigny and Berlaimont 1829–30).
- **1867–1883:** the Treaty of London made Luxembourg neutral and the fortress was dismantled. Surface works were blown up and buried, but most galleries were too dangerous to destroy under the town: openings and embrasures were walled up and the network left in place.

**Numbers usually quoted:** about **23 km** of galleries (some sources say "nearly 25 km") connecting over **40,000 m²** of bomb-proof space; about **17 km survive**; dug on **several levels**, reaching **up to 40 m** below the surface. The Bock and Pétrusse casemates opened to visitors on **22 July 1933** and are part of the UNESCO World Heritage site (1994). In both world wars they served as air-raid shelters for about **35,000 people**.

| Where | Bock promontory, east edge of the Ville Haute above the Alzette (Grund / Clausen). Entrance Montée de Clausen 10; OSM node `Casemates du Bock` (49.6117, 6.1367, `level=-1`). |
| Built | Medieval castle cellars (castle of Count Siegfried, 963); first casemates 1644 (Spanish); remodelled by Vauban after 1684; main works by the Austrians 1737–1746 (enlarged by Neipperg c. 1744–45). Castle bridge (Pont du Château / Schlassbréck) 1735. |

## Pétrusse casemates (Casemates de la Pétrusse, Péitruss-Kasematten)

| | |
|---|---|
| Where | Under the Beck bastion (Place de la Constitution, Gëlle Fra) and the Beck-Jost ravelin ("Paschtéitchen", built 1673/74), descending to the Pétrusse valley. Entrance next to the kiosk by the Gëlle Fra; OSM node `Pétrusse Casemates` (49.6094, 6.1299). |
| Built | 1644 gallery dug in the medieval ditch; shaped by Vauban; Austrian "Batterie de la Pétrusse" artillery casemates under the Paschtéitchen **1746–1751**. The "Austrian staircase" inside the bastion led down to the Bourbon lock (1728–29, Simon de Bauffe) and through it up to Fort Bourbon: a continuous underground link between inner and outer works. |
| Route | About **450 m**: 23 steps down eastwards, then south; the "small stair" (43 steps, emergency exit to the stair down to the Louis-Beck fausse-braie); an **86 m gallery** running west under the whole Place de la Constitution (both ends once had posterns); halfway along, under the Gëlle Fra, the "great" or "Austrian" stair (**131 steps**) goes south to the valley (closed, emergency exit only); a chicane with a murder hole; 31 steps down west under the Beck-Jost fausse-braie (light show room); 16 up; 52 down into the Paschtéitchen ravelin (emergency exit to the footpath); gun room with embrasures over the valley; 51 more down to the room of "Cannon 31" (1834, the only surviving Prussian-era gun); exit up 7 steps at the south-west tip of the ravelin behind the "Priedegtstull", into the Pétrusse valley. Some sources count 242 steps in all. |
| History of use | Shooting range of the Société de tir from 1871; mushroom growing in the 1890s; fairs and bazaars early 1900s; air-raid shelter in both world wars; opened to tourists 22 July 1933 (5,000 visitors in the first season). |
| Visiting | Closed 2015 for safety work; reopened **5 June 2022** with a sound-and-light scenography. Guided tours only (1 h, max 12, booked through the LCTO; adults €24 with guide in 2026). Not suitable for reduced mobility. |
| Sources | [lb.wikipedia: Péitruss-Kasematten](https://lb.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A9itruss-Kasematten), [LCTO tour page](https://www.luxembourg-city.com/en/place/tour/petrusse-casemates) |

<a id="grunewald"></a>
## Grünewald front: Fort Thüngen, Obergrünewald, Niedergrünewald, Olizy

North-east of the old town, on the Kirchberg side of Pfaffenthal. The **Hiel** is the small valley between the Obergrünewald and Niedergrünewald works (once the line of the Roman road Arlon–Trier); the galleries of this front run on both sides of it.

**Fort Thüngen / Dräi Eechelen** (OSM way 26424184, 49.6165, 6.1394; 324 m a.s.l.)
- Vauban's small "Redoute du Parc" (1688); the fort proper built **1732–33** by Simon de Bauffe and named after the commander Adam Sigmund von Thüngen; extended by the Prussians in **1836–37** (the three round towers with acorns) and **1859–60**.
- Keep (réduit) **96 m deep, about 953 m², one storey**, heavily mined; 20–23 loopholes; an envelope with casemated flanks, a deep ditch and two lunettes.
- Access by a covered way from Fort Obergrünewald and by a **169 m casemate gallery** that came out in the middle tower at the rear.
- Its mine system was the most extensive of the fortress: **33 galleries with 71 mine chambers**.
- The keep's underground parts survived the 1870s demolition almost whole; rebuilt in the 1990s with the Mudam, opened **2012 as Musée Dräi Eechelen** (permanent exhibition in casemates 2–10).
- Sources: [lb.wikipedia: Fort Thüngen](https://lb.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Th%C3%BCngen), [m3e.public.lu](https://m3e.public.lu/en/galeries/fort-thuengen.html).

**Fort Obergrünewald** (OSM way 49908579, 49.6153, 6.1376) – Vauban hornwork, 1684–85 and 1688; razed after 1867 and buried under an esplanade by Édouard André; partly reconstructed (gorge, covered way, postern and caponier, powder magazine) by the Service des Sites et Monuments in the park designed by Michel Desvigne. OSM has information boards for the postern and caponier.

**Fort Niedergrünewald** (OSM way 52345116, 49.6163, 6.1339) – Vauban crownwork above Pfaffenthal; almost entirely gone, some casemates remain; OSM marks a private cave entrance "Entrée Fort Niedergrünewald" (49.6172, 6.1357).

**Fort Olizy** (OSM node 5459427573, 49.6187, 6.1368) – Vauban, after 1684; irregular pentagon facing the Alzette, between today's Avenue J.F. Kennedy and the European Investment Bank. Its casemates and the connecting galleries to Fort Thüngen were partly destroyed when the Red Bridge was built (1960s). A new access was built during the tram works on Avenue J.F. Kennedy (photo `olizy-casemates__new-access.jpg`); OSM has a private entrance "Entrée Fort Olizy" (49.6190, 6.1363). Closed to the public.

**3D:** the Musée Dräi Eechelen published a **Matterport 3D walk-through of the Olizy and Niedergrünewald casemates**: https://my.matterport.com/show/?m=KFRATurWNoP (link only; the best way to see the inside of these galleries). The MNAHA fortress documentation centre also scanned these galleries and Fort Thüngen (2018); presented at https://collections.mnaha.lu/stories/casemates (blocks automated access; not checked).

## Plain front: Louvigny, Berlaimont, Marie, Lambert

The western "Front de la Plaine", now the Municipal Park, Boulevard Royal and Avenue Monterey. In 1672–73 the Spanish engineer Louvigny put a ring of infantry towers ("redoutes") in front of the bastions: Peter, Louvigny, Marie, Berlaimont. All were razed and buried in 1869–71.

- **Fort Louvigny** (49.6114, 6.1229): redoubt 1672–73; envelope 1733 and 1739 by Simon de Bauffe ("Fort Daun"); Prussian work 1829–30 and 1859; razed 1869; **Villa Louvigny (1871) stands on the redoubt's base**. MNAHA 3D scan, **CC BY 4.0 on Sketchfab** (2.0 M triangles, made with RealityCapture by in-visible): https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/4b2cf11b951149299f170dd139665908, also on Europeana as CC0: https://www.europeana.eu/item/1130/share3d_1557. Download needs a (free) Sketchfab account, so it is link-only here.
- **Fort Berlaimont** (49.6156, 6.1286): redoubt 1672–73 of about 75 m² on **two casemated storeys** linked by a stair; reached by a caponier on one side and an **underground gallery to the main ditch** on the other; envelope 1739; renovated 1829–30; dismantled 1871. Remains run under the Municipal Park and the Fondation Pescatore. **Air-raid shelter in WWII** (but not usable when General Patton had his HQ in the Fondation Pescatore in 1944–45). Source: [FFGL](https://www.ffgl.lu/forteresse/berlaimont/fr_index.html).
- **Redoute Marie** (49.6133, 6.1260): casemate below the Fondation Pescatore (photo `redoute-marie-casemates__interior.jpg`); OSM cave entrance "Bastion Marie".
- **Fort Lambert** (49.6100, 6.1236): covered with earth after 1867 and Avenue Monterey built over it. In **2001** the construction of the **Monterey underground car park** uncovered one of its redoubts and its caponier, which are kept visible inside the car park (OSM: `Redoute Lambert` layer -2, `Caponière du fort Lambert` layer -2, `Bastion du fort Lambert` relation). FFGL leaflet on Lambert and Berlaimont: https://www.ffgl.lu/forteresse/lambert/fr_depllamberl.pdf (link only).
- OSM also has cave-entrance nodes for **Fort Vauban** (49.6127, 6.1242) and **Fort Royal** (49.6150, 6.1263).

<a id="southwest"></a>
## South-western front: Bourbon, Wallis and others

- **Fort Bourbon** (49.6073, 6.1287, Gare side of the Pétrusse): connected to the Beck bastion through the Bourbon lock gallery and the Austrian staircase (see Pétrusse). Casemates survive (photos `bourbon-casemates__*.jpg`), not open.
- **Fort Wallis** (49.6040, 6.1345): casemate entrance on the Montée de la Pétrusse (photo).
- **Fort Élisabeth, Fort Rheinsheim, Fort Wedell, Fort Neipperg**: OSM points only. **Fort Wedell** and **Fort Neipperg** have given their names to car parks (Fort Wedell underground, 420 places; Fort Neipperg multi-storey, 536 places) built on their sites.
- **Fort Dumoulin** (Clausen / Rham side, 49.6132, 6.1508): a casemate was filled with concrete by INS workers (photo `fort-dumoulin__casemate-concrete.jpg`).

## Rham plateau, Grund, Pfaffenthal and the Wenzel wall

- **Wenzel wall (Mur de Wenceslas, c. 1390)** runs from the Bock down through the Grund to the Rham plateau; the third ring of the medieval walls. It is a surface wall with towers, gates (Grund gate, Jacob tower) and wall walks; OSM way `Mur de Wenceslas` (831169742). Beside it, OSM has an underground gun emplacement (`military=bunker`, `bunker_type=gun_emplacement`, layer -1, way 546345995, 49.6118, 6.1373) and an underground wall section (way 546346021) on the Bock / Montée de Clausen side.
- **Rham front**: the FFGL lists an underground "Galerie d'accès (Rham)" (https://www.ffgl.lu/forteresse/galerierham/fr_index.html, photos only, no text). The Lefort map detail `rham-front__lefort-1794-detail.jpg` shows the works.
- **Grund**: the Grund battery of the Bock casemates (point G of the visit) covered the lower town; the Écluse du Grund (lock, OSM relation 20986707) closed the Alzette. The Saint-Esprit citadel above the Grund holds the Grund lift (see below).
- **Pfaffenthal**: the valley was closed by the Vauban/Austrian walls and the "Béinchen" bridge; the galleries on this side belong to the Niedergrünewald, Olizy and Bock systems. No separate visitable Pfaffenthal gallery was found.
- The **1777 plan of the galleries and mines** (`fortress-underground__plan-souterrains-mines-1777.jpg`) is the best single source for galleries on all these fronts: its legend says main and communication galleries are drawn as black-and-red dotted lines, main mines in black and branch mines in red.

<a id="wwii"></a>
## WWII air-raid shelters

During both world wars the Bock and Pétrusse casemates were used as air-raid shelters, with room for about **35,000 people** (figure quoted by the Tourist Office). The Berlaimont galleries under the Municipal Park were also used as a shelter. The Aquatunnel was fitted out as a Civil Protection nuclear shelter after it was built in the 1960s (FFGL). No plan of the wartime shelter layout was found.

## Aquatunnel

| | |
|---|---|
| What | Rock-cut gallery under the old town from the **Pétrusse valley to Pfaffenthal**, built **1961–1963** to carry waste water from the southern quarters towards the Beggen treatment plant; also fitted out as a Civil Protection nuclear shelter. |
| Size | About **900 m** long (960 m in one source); about 4.40 m wide and 3.25 m high; **60–64 m below** the city centre. Signs inside mark what is overhead (Côte d'Eich, Puits Rouge, Place d'Armes). |
| Shafts | An old fortress well, the **Place d'Armes well** (about 65 m), can be seen from the tunnel (photo `aquatunnel__shaft-place-darmes.jpg`). |
| Visiting | Normally closed; occasional guided visits and event openings (e.g. City Jogging once ran through it; LUGA 2025). |
| Sources | [FFGL](https://www.ffgl.lu/forteresse/tunnel/fr_index.html), [pfaffenthal.info](http://www.pfaffenthal.info/2013/07/den-aquatunnel.html), [Chronicle.lu](https://chronicle.lu/category/things-to-see-do/55076-aquatunnel-provides-access-to-luxembourg-citys-hidden-underground-secrets) |

<a id="road-tunnels"></a>
## Road tunnels

- **Tunnel René Konen** (Saint-Esprit tunnel, Helleg-Geescht-Tunnel), N57: **638.5 m**, two lanes one-way northbound, about 25,000 vehicles a day. South portal under the Cité judiciaire (fed by the Passerelle from the Gare side), bends under the Chamber of Deputies and comes out on the Alzette side, splitting to Boulevard Royal and Côte d'Eich. Planned from the early 1970s, inaugurated by Grand Duke Jean on **18 June 1988**, named after the minister René Konen in 1998. OSM way 52065179 (`layer=-3`). Photo `tunnel-rene-konen__entrance.jpg`.
- **"Tunnel Grand-Duc Jean"**: no road tunnel of this name was found. The René Konen tunnel was the one opened by Grand Duke Jean; the name may have been confused with it.
- **Tunnel Albert Bousser** (Rocade de Bonnevoie, B3, about 870 m outline in OSM, south of the Gare) and **Tunnel Cents** (A1 Autoroute de Trèves, about 680 m, east of Kirchberg) are in the OSM extract; both are outside the old town.
- Shorter road underpasses: Rond-Point Robert Schuman (layer -2), Boulevard Konrad Adenauer and the Desserte Schuman at Kirchberg, Rue du Fort Thüngen.

<a id="rail-tunnels"></a>
## Rail tunnels

- **North head of Luxembourg station**: OSM maps a set of tracks named `Tunnel tête Nord gare Luxembourg` (`layer=-2`, line 3, about 1 km of track segments between 49.6025, 6.1360 and 49.6065, 6.1383), part of the CFL project of the second Pulvermühl viaduct and the station extension that added a single-track tunnel north of the station to remove the north-head bottleneck. Geometry in OSM is "estimated from photos and videos", so treat it as approximate.
- **Pfaffenthal-Kirchberg**: the funicular runs partly in tunnel (below). The railway on Line 10 through Pfaffenthal and Pulvermühl crosses the valleys on viaducts; short rail tunnel segments appear in OSM at Pfaffenthal (49.6188, 6.1329) and at Hollerich / Cessange.

<a id="lifts"></a>
## Lifts and funicular

- **Pfaffenthal panoramic lift** (Lift Pafendall–Uewerstad), opened **22 July 2016**: 60 m open shaft against a 74.8 m concrete tower set in a 20 m deep cutting of the Côte d'Eich cliff; a 72 m enclosed footbridge links the top to the Pescatore park. Not underground, but a vertical link to draw. OSM node `Lift Pfaffenthal` (49.6155, 6.1306).
- **Grund lift** (Ascenseur du Grund, Plateau du Saint-Esprit): from the Saint-Esprit plateau down to Rue Saint-Ulric in the Grund; at the bottom a **pedestrian tunnel through the rock** leads to the lift (photos `grund-lift__*.jpg`). OSM node `Ascenseur du Grund` (49.6079, 6.1344).
- **Pfaffenthal-Kirchberg funicular** (CFL), opened **10 December 2017**: two parallel lines, each with two cars of 168 people; about **200 m long, 38.7 m rise, 19.7 % slope**, 63 s per trip. The upper part runs in a cut-and-cover tunnel (OSM `tunnel=yes`, `layer=-1`, about 80 m) under Avenue J.F. Kennedy to the Rout Bréck–Pafendall tram stop.

Notes on the OSM data:
- **Casemate galleries themselves are almost not mapped in OSM.** Only a few fortress features carry `layer=-1/-2` (Fort Lambert redoubt and caponier, a gun emplacement on the Bock, one gallery line), plus point entrances (`natural=cave_entrance`) for Lambert, Louvigny, Berlaimont, Vauban, Royal, Bastion Marie, Olizy and Niedergrünewald, and museum points for the Bock (`level=-1`) and Pétrusse casemates. The galleries have to be drawn from the historical plans.
- `pedestrian_tunnel` (283) and `service_tunnel_or_parking_ramp` (113) are mostly building passages, car-park ramps and short underpasses; filter by `tunnel=yes` and length before drawing.
- `water_culvert` features are streams in culverts (the Pétrusse and side streams are partly piped).
- The Aquatunnel is not in OSM.
- The extract was built from nine Overpass sub-queries (tunnels, negative layers, historic, military, names, parking, lifts/funicular, caves/man_made tunnels, entrances); building entrances were left out.

<a id="recommendation"></a>
## Recommendation: building an underground layer

1. **Base geometry:** georeference `fortress__overlay-1794-on-modern-map.png` (already aligned to a modern street map) or Lefort's 1794 plan against `unesco_existing_fortress.geojson` and the OSM fortress features. Surviving walls and the OSM entrance points give dozens of control points.
2. **Galleries and countermines:** trace them from the two plans that actually draw them: the **French "Plan de Luxembourg et des Contre-mines" (1794–95)**, which is large (about 9000 px) and clear, and the **Austrian "Plan des souterrains et mines" (c. 1777)**, which has the better legend (main/communication galleries vs. main and branch mines) but is only 2400 px on Commons. The 1794–95 plan draws every countermine system as grey galleries with T-shaped branches around the plain-front redoubts, the Grünewald forts (lettered A–D), Fort Charles (X) and the southern works (I–L), with a lettered legend. Use the 1741 redoubt countermine plan as the template for a single fort: plan **and section** at 1:240 with a legend (N main gallery, O branches "rameaux", P shafts, Q closures, R mine chambers "fourneaux", I underground communication, K/M descending entrances), which gives real gallery widths and depths to model.
3. **Visitable systems in detail:** Bock from the LCTO leaflet plan (A–I) and the facts above; Pétrusse from the step-by-step route description (stairs and gallery lengths are given); Olizy/Niedergrünewald from the Matterport tour; Louvigny from the CC BY Sketchfab model.
4. **Modern layer:** take road and rail tunnels, the funicular, lifts and car parks straight from `osm_underground_luxembourg_city.geojson` (`category` = `road_tunnel`, `rail_tunnel`, `lift_funicular`, `parking_underground`). The Aquatunnel has to be drawn by hand (Pétrusse valley to Pfaffenthal, passing under Place d'Armes at about 60 m depth).
5. **Depth:** for an isometric cut-away, a simple scheme works: car parks and road tunnels 5–20 m, casemates in tiers down to about 40 m (Bock well 47 m), Aquatunnel 60–65 m.

<a id="gaps"></a>
## Gaps

- **No free, georeferenced plan of the surviving 17 km of galleries exists.** The modern survey is held by the Service des Sites et Monuments / INPA and the MNAHA fortress documentation centre and is not published openly; the 2013 *Kasematten* book is the best printed source.
- The 1777 gallery plan (drawn by Michael Wagner, gunner of the 2nd company) is only 2400 px wide on Commons; it comes from the Musée Dräi Eechelen's "object of the month" (February 2018, m3e.lu), which holds the original and could supply a higher-resolution scan.
- Gallica could not be searched by script, and eluxemburgensia, DDB and the Prussian archives were not searched; more 19th-century Prussian mine plans almost certainly exist there.
- No plan of the Pétrusse casemates route, of the WWII shelter layout or of the Aquatunnel alignment was found (descriptions only).
- The Hiel and Pfaffenthal galleries have no separate documentation online beyond the historical plans.
- OSM geometry of the station's north-head rail tunnel is an estimate.
- MNAHA 3D scans of Thüngen and Olizy could not be checked (site blocks automated access).
